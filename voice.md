# voice.md: HarShitified

Faceless Shorts channel. Every youtube-agent skill reads this. Daily runs copy it to ~/.claude/youtube/voice.md.

## Who I am talking to
One person scrolling Shorts at night: 16-30, loves short, twisty, genuinely creepy horror stories about strange towns with strange
rules ("in this town..."). They will swipe in two seconds unless the first line is impossible to ignore.

## How I actually talk
The kid at the centre of the story, telling it in first person ("I"): what happened to me in my strange town. Quiet,
eerie and unsettling, with a little dry humour early on and real fear by the end; most stories end open, with the threat
still out there. Short punchy sentences, but they flow ("See, ...", "But here's the thing...",
"That's when I understood..."). Simple everyday words. The AI narrator (am_michael) reads every word.
Example shape: "In this town, you never hum after dark. My little sister hums all the time."

## Words I never use
"In this video", "let's dive in", "make sure to like and subscribe", "game-changer", "mind-blowing", "insane",
"you won't believe", "stay tuned", "unlock". No channel intro, no outro, no subscribe pitch.

## Words I do use
Numbers said out loud ("four thousand times"), counted escalation ("Day three. Day five."), sound words on cuts
("Poof.", "Boom."), "See,", "That's when I understood", "Nobody told me the rule."

## What I will not claim
The stories are ORIGINAL FICTION, never presented as true: no "this really happened", no real people, brands, games or
real tragedies, no fake news framing. No plots, lines, characters or monsters copied from other creators. Spooky, never gory.

## My format
Vertical Shorts, under 2 minutes (usually 95-115 s), told at a natural storytelling pace (voice speed 1.0) with real pauses after reveals and around dialogue. Never rushed to fit a time limit: let the build-up breathe. Faceless: Block Town 3D
visuals, short captions, new shot every 0.8-1.8 s. First person, the lead's point of view. Opens on the town's ONE
bizarre rule ("In this town, ..."). Follows story-refs/PLAYBOOK.md: weird town details, someone I love at stake, the rule
breaks, counted escalation, a twist that shows the town had it wrong (paid off by an early detail), and a funny or
creepy final beat.
